# 12. Scale and the ceiling

## 12.1 The numbers

- **Target per school:** 50–500 students reachable via middlemen.
- **Realistic first year:** tens of users, a few hundred dollars a month.
- **Designed ceiling:** a single school.

## 12.2 The free tier raises the ceiling, and the floor

The free tier changes what "scale" means here, in both directions:

- **It raises the headcount ceiling.** A free tier can plausibly reach
  *most* of a school, where a $2 floor could not.
- **It lowers revenue per head** by roughly half
  ([`05-pricing.md`](05-pricing.md#541-how-this-compares-to-the-old-ladder)).
- **So the bet is headcount over margin** — and it only pays if free users
  convert ([`11-growth.md`](11-growth.md#113-the-free-tier-as-the-acquisition-engine)).

At 1,000 users (65% free), the ladder produces roughly **$2,500/mo net**
([`05-pricing.md`](05-pricing.md#54-sensitivity)). That is a real number at
pocket-money scale, and still not a business that rewards chasing 10× effort.

## 12.3 Growth beyond one school

Growth beyond a single school needs:

- more middlemen (recruiting, trusting, and managing more people),
- more support (more students, more problems),
- eventually a second VPS (more cost, and a data-consistency problem
  between hubs).

Margins shrink as you scale while effort rises. This is the reason the plan
is written for one school in the first place.

## 12.4 The honest ambition

> **This is a side project that pays for itself and a bit more.** A version
> that makes a few hundred dollars a month and covers its costs is a
> *success*, not a stalled startup. It does not need 10× growth, investors,
> or a roadmap.

**Low exit cost is a feature.** If it stops being worth it, the VPS bill
ends and so does the business — no inventory, no debt, no legal
entanglements.

## 12.5 What would change this file

- **A second school** would break every single-school assumption in this
  directory, including the free tier's bandwidth economics.
- **Measured conversion data** ([`16-metrics.md`](16-metrics.md)) would turn
  the sensitivity table from an assumption into a forecast.
